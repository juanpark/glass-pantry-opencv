Glass Pantry

A fridge you can open on your phone, kept true by photos. You photograph each shelf, Claude reads what is on it, and OpenCV 5 checks the photos and the readings and decides what needs a second look. You confirm everything. Next time you photograph a shelf, OpenCV compares it with the last photo, so the model is asked only about what may have changed (experimental).

The idea

The fridge on the screen is the record; photos keep it true. A model reading a photo can be confidently wrong, and reading every shelf again every time is slow and costly. So the model only proposes. OpenCV supplies the evidence that decides the next step: send the photo back, ask you, or file. And when you come back, OpenCV decides which spots are worth reading again.

What we measured

Each bar was written down before the test photos were looked at. One kitchen, few photos: these show behaviour, not general accuracy.

  • Photograph it again (6 new before/after pairs): every removal, the moved item and the added item were found. It still failed its bar on false alarms: 8 things that had not changed were asked about (bar: at most 1 per pair) and 7 spots were wrongly marked as new. Classic SIFT matching under the same rules: 38 and 12. So it is labelled experimental, and the shelf-strip setup the page now uses was not part of the test.
  • Second looks at small labels (12 scans of new photos, 28 scored readings): no first reading was wrong, so there was nothing to catch; 2 questions were raised for nothing. No accuracy gain is claimed.
  • Suggested corners and snapping edges: failed their bar and are switched off; you place the corners and lines.
  • Speed on AWS, comparing two photos, OpenCV only: 4.4 s, 9.0 s on the first call (one pair, a spot check).

Before you try it

  • Photos you send are processed by this engine on AWS, in memory, without being intentionally kept; it logs only codes and counts. When a model reading is made (a shelf, the fridge's sticker, or the marked spots you ask to be read), that photo or those crops also go to Anthropic's Claude model; this page does not set Anthropic's retention policy. Tracing an outline and the first comparison of two photos use AWS only. Leave people and personal documents out of the frame.
  • Your fridge is kept in this browser tab only.
  • Reading photos is capped per day for everyone together, so live reading can run out; the recorded shelf check always works.
